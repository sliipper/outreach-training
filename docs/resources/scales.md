# Scales at LHCb
Useful to provide scales to help audience understand and visualise what you are explaining. The CERN [Large Hadron Collider](https://home.cern/science/accelerators/large-hadron-collider/) webpage has many useful facts and figures for the LHC. 

Additional information on other parts of the accelerator complex can be found at the [CERN Visit Point guiding pages](https://guides.web.cern.ch/guided-tours).

## LHCb statistics
- LHCb has collected 37 fb$^{-1}$ of data up to the end of Run 3, see [operations webpages](https://lbgroups.cern.ch/online/operations_plots/) 

- The whole dataset is a roughly 200 petabytes, this is the same as 100 million 2-hour movies!

- The whole LHCb detector weighs roughly 5600 tonnes, this is about the same as the fully-fuelled SpaceX Starship!

- LHCb sits ~104m underground, this is further below ground than the height of Big Ben or the Statue of Liberty.

- LHC proton beam energy, 13.6 TeV is an energy equivalent to a fly!

- The LHC collides bunches of protons 40 million times per second, so there are 25 nano seconds between the collisions. This means roughly 10 million collisions happen in the time it takes you to blink!
  
- Each proton bunch in the LHC contains $~10^{11}$ protons. This is about the same number of protons as there are stars in our galaxy! And we have about 2800 of these bunches in each of the two beams in the LHC.

- The VELO can measure the primary vertex position, the place where our $b$-hadrons are created, to within the width of a human hair

- LHCb is 20m long, equivalent to two double-decker London buses end-to-end, around 5 African Bush Elephants or a Sperm Whale! 

- The LHCb magnets each weigh 27 tonnes, which is roughly equivalent to 23 giraffes.


## Delphi statistics
- Delphi public site: https://delphi-www.web.cern.ch/delphi-www/
- Delphi = DEtector with Lepton, Photon and Hadron Identification.
- LEP, compared to the LHC's 2800 bunches per beam, only had 4 bunches per beam!
- On average, they only produced one Z boson every 2 seconds (due to the low e+e- -> Z cross section).
- Delphi's superconducting magnet was the largest SC magnet built at the time and had only 10cm clearance on the access shaft.
- Delphi weighed 3,200 tonnes - just over half the weight of LHCb!
